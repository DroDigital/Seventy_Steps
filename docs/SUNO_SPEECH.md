# Suno Speech: the intro cards

Suno Speech (public beta, launched October 1, 2026) makes a spoken voice and an original score together as one track. In **Advanced Mode** you paste your own script in one box and, in a second, "describe the delivery, tone, pacing, mood and setting". Sources: [Suno Speech Beta](https://www.aimusicpreneur.com/ai-tools-news/suno-speech-beta-spoken-word-music-one-take-launch/), [How to Make Suno Speak](https://jackrighteous.com/en-us/blogs/guides-using-suno-ai-music-creation/suno-ai-spoken-narration-workflow). Suno warns that accents can drift and dramatic pauses can run long in the beta, so generate a few takes per card and keep the steadiest. Use the same delivery prompt for every card so the voice stays one voice.

Note: the game's own recordings use Eleven v4 [audio tags]. Suno does not read those, so the scripts below are the plain words with pauses written as "..." and paragraph breaks. Words in capitals in the game (READING, ELSE) are stressed words: kept as italics-free plain words here, the prompt asks for the stress.

## The delivery prompt (paste in the second box, for every card)

> A single male narrator, crisp, authoritative British voice, mature and measured, like a 1920s scholar reading from his own notebook. Low and steady, never whispered, never sung, no rhythm or rhyme. Slow, unhurried pacing with long pauses at full stops and the ellipses; a grave, quietly dread-laden mood that tightens a little at the last line of each passage. Setting: a dim, empty library at night in Arkham, Massachusetts, 1928, one lamp lit, a distant clock ticking. Very sparse score underneath: a low sustained drone and a faint cold string or organ swell, far behind the voice, no drums, no melody, no vocals.

## The cards (paste in the first box, one card at a time)

**1. Telegram (typed, flat and urgent, then grave)**
```
Telegram. Arkham, Massachusetts. October third, nineteen twenty-eight.

Come at once.

Peaslee found asleep in the library Monday, and cannot be woken.

Six more in town, the same.

Will meet the Boston train.

Armitage.
```

**2. From the notebook · October 5th**
```
From the notebook. October fifth.

Eleven sleepers now, two of them children. Their eyes move under the lids, as if they were reading.

All of them speak of the same things: a stair going down... a key... and a tall man in a good coat.
```

**3. October 6th**
```
October sixth.

Armitage thinks Peaslee read what he should have left alone, and that the sleepers are not so much asleep as somewhere else.

He wants me to go after them.
```

**4. October 6th, later**
```
October sixth, later.

He drew me the sign the old towns cut over their doors, and said that wherever I find it, I may rest.

He gave me a lamp.

I did not ask what it was for.
```

**5. October 6th, late**
```
October sixth, late.

I lay down on a bench in the reading room, a little after eleven, and heard the clock strike.

When I opened my eyes, the lamps were out... and beyond the windows lay a town I did not know.
```

Tip: if a take rushes, add "very slow" to the end of the prompt; if the score is too loud, add "score barely audible, under the voice".
