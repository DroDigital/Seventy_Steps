## Long description

**Seventy Steps** is a Lovecraftian open-world soulslike in the look of the first 3D games: billboarded sprites in low-poly worlds,
dithered fog, a low-resolution picture, and a night that is meant to be dark.

You are an investigator in Arkham, October 1928. The dream is open to anyone who goes down the stairs. It is a world of thirteen
regions: Miskatonic University, Arkham and the Blasted Heath, Dunwich and the Round Hills, Innsmouth and Devil Reef, Providence and
Kingsport, the Vermont hills, the Mountains of Madness, the archives of Pnakotus, K'n-yan and N'kai, the Dreamlands, Mu and R'lyeh, Yuggoth,
and the gate beyond, with the court at the centre of everything.

- **A soulslike with a mind to lose.** Dodge, block, parry and riposte with a sword-cane; fire a six-shot revolver; heal with West's
  Reagent. Your sanity drains in the dark, at what you see and at what is said, and a failing mind changes the world: wrong stars,
  whispers, horrors that were never there. Laudanum and lamplight bring it back.
- **Forty-seven horrors.** From the Dunwich Horror and Dr. Muñoz to Cthulhu, Hastur, Yog-Sothoth and Azathoth, each in its own
  arena, with its own music and its own demand beyond striking: a blind god that hears you, a ship to drive, powder to scatter, a
  gaze to hide from.
- **An open world with places to rest.** Light Elder Signs, rest, grow stronger with the Echoes you carry (lose them at a fall and fetch
  them back), and travel between the signs. A candle before each horror's fog lets you rise beside it after a fall, or at the sign.
- **A hundred creatures, a hundred ways to die.** Packs that take turns, things that cannot be seen, things that wait in the ground.
- **People and what they know.** Quests, tomes and notes, and strangers who tell you a little, if you listen.
- **Three difficulties**, chosen once as a dream begins: Light Slumber, Deep Slumber, Nightmare. A second journey with harder
  horrors. Three endings.
- **Made to be read.** Rebindable keys, pad support with the prompts of the device in your hand, a cap on every effect of a failing
  mind, screen-shake and brightness settings, cutscenes you may skip, photo mode (P).

## Content notes (for the store's questionnaire)

Violence against creatures and people (stylised, low-resolution); cosmic and psychological horror; no blood-and-gore realism; no
nudity; no real-world gambling. Screen effects (warping, flashes of lightning, a failing mind's distortions) can be capped under
Settings › Play › FX intensity. Say it on the page.

## AI-generated content disclosure (Steam requires it for pre-generated content)

State on the store page, in the AI disclosure section:
- **Voices:** the spoken lines of the people and the horrors are synthetic speech, made with ElevenLabs (Eleven v4) from the game's
  own script (`docs/VOICES.md`). One of them, Morgan, uses the ElevenLabs library voice Josef Hammer.
- **Music:** the fifteen realm tracks, the forty-eight boss themes and the title theme were made with Suno (`public/music/CREDITS.md`).
- **Code:** written with the help of Claude Code (Anthropic).
- **Not AI:** every texture, sprite, mesh and effect is generated by the game's own code, not by an image model. The recorded sound
  effects are CC0 recordings (`public/audio/CREDITS.md`).

**Before release, check the licences yourself:** that the Suno plan used when each track was made allows commercial release; that the
ElevenLabs plan allows it; and the licence and notice period of the library voice Josef Hammer on its library page (the tools used
here could not show it). This file records what was used; it is not legal advice.
