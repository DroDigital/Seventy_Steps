## The program

Replace every creature drawn in 2D with a PS3-grade 3D model generated by code. That is the 93 enemies, their 12 variants
and the 7 allies: 112 forms, today in `public/creatures/reviewed` and `public/creatures/allies`, the bosses included.

Each form gets lore-accurate anatomy, skinned animation for every move it makes, cinematic effects, and a move set
redesigned from its lore and anatomy.

The kickoff builds everything the forms share and proves it on four. The batches then add data, not machinery, and run as
parallel sessions.

## The bar: PS3-grade fidelity

- **Faithful anatomy:** what the text says, at the size it says, modelled with real form (muscle, folds, chitin plates, wet
  membranes), not primitives.
- **Clear silhouette:** reads from 5 to 30 m, and is unlike every other form's.
- **Well-made motion:** smooth skinned bodies; locomotion blended by speed and turn; anticipation, weight, follow-through,
  and overlapping loose parts on spring bones; planted contacts; an idle with character.
- **Polished textures, generated in code:** albedo, normal, roughness or specular, emissive and AO, filtered and mipmapped,
  with detail normals baked from each form's high-detail shape onto its game mesh. No seams, mud or tiling.
- **Material response:** per-pixel lighting from the moon, lantern and lamps; wet sheen, chitin specular, subsurface warmth
  in flesh and membranes, rim light.
- **Cinematic juice:**
  - wind-up glow on the striking part, trails;
  - impact flashes, and sparks and ichor where struck;
  - hitstop and camera punch by weight;
  - debris and decals, and emitters on the body;
  - layered deaths, arrivals and phase changes.
- **Well composited:**
  - real shadows cast and received, keeping round 94's long, looming cast;
  - grounded by contact shadow and footfall dust, parting the mist and the grass;
  - graded with its realm and fogged as the world is;
  - particles layered and blended in depth order with the fog;
  - nothing floating, sliding or clipping, and the camera never inside a body.
- **Readable and fair:** juice never hides a telegraph, and it obeys the FX intensity cap, the shake and flash settings,
  and foeCrowd's particle budget.

The creatures have a look of their own: they need not match the investigator's figure.

## The picture (my decision, after the pilot)

## Rules this amends

I am asking for these. Log them in DECISIONS, and amend SPEC §0, §2 and §3B and CLAUDE.md's out-of-scope line to match.

- **What changes:** creatures are PS3-grade 3D, not PS1 billboards, and skeletal animation is in scope for them.
- **How they are made:** they are modelled, textured, rigged and animated in TypeScript, from small seeded recipes in data.
  No Blender, no Python, no baked or generated data files, no asset files.
- **The modelling kit** (render side):
  - signed-distance sculpting (blends, creases, folds, plates), meshed by surface nets at high detail, then decimated to
    each LOD;
  - lofts and lathes;
  - normal and AO maps baked in code from the high-detail shape onto the game mesh;
  - UVs from the generators, triplanar where a surface has none.
- **Textures** are generated in code, on the GPU at load (procedural shaders rendered to textures): packed albedo/roughness
  and normal/AO maps, and emissive where a form glows.
- **Rigs:**
  - smooth skinning, ≤4 weights per vertex, the weights computed in code;
  - ≤64 bones (≤128 for colossi);
  - tentacles, tails and serpents as bone chains, and loose parts on spring bones;
  - formless bodies (shoggoths, blobs, the Colour, spectres, the Outer Gods' masses) also deform in the vertex shader, from
    parameters their clips key per frame.
- **Motion** is authored and blended: crossfades, blends of locomotion and additive hit reactions. IK only plants feet on
  the real ground and aims heads and eyes. No procedural lean, bob or breath beyond what the clips key (round 83).
- **Animation:** port tools/player's clip DSL and solver (`clipkit.py` and `solve.py`: numpy, no Blender) to TypeScript,
  keeping its markers and QA. Signature moves are keyed with it; gaits and reactions are generated from the rig.
- **Material:** a new creature material (a ShaderMaterial, like every material here) that still takes the world's shared
  uniforms: fog, realm grade, the sanity FX and the lantern.
- **Generation** is deterministic and seeded. A realm's forms are built in workers and on the GPU during loading, and
  cached by recipe hash (IndexedDB).
- **Dependencies:** ask before adding any new npm dependency.

## The data stays the authority (the simulation imports no Three.js)

- **Timing:** hit windows, spheres, damage and stamina are the data's. Clips conform to them through markers, as
  playerClips do (hit0/hit1/shot/item…, retimed by clipTime through each boss's bossPunish band).
- **Capsules** and reachOf stay. The model fits its capsule within about 10%; changing a capsule is a balance change.
- **Sockets** (mouth, hands or claws, eyes, tips, feet) and a colossus's hurt zones come from the same rig, as numbers per
  clip frame. They are computed without Three.js, so systems/hurt.ts and the effects can read them.
- **Move sets are data:** the shared library (a new library attack only when three or more forms use it), or a per-entity
  override that validateRegistry checks. Signatures stay the only bespoke logic (spec §3E). Keep the bossPunish bands,
  BOSS.blowMost and round 100's summon caps.
- **Look rules:** keep every rule the sprites earned (DECISIONS 87 and 93–96):
  - the realm grade's share, lamplight warmth, the lantern's reveal along the wheel, the roof rule;
  - strongly coloured forms as made, anomaly hues on glow only, the Colour outside the palette;
  - the Outer Gods' echoes, eyes that glow from the dark, the glitch of hallucinations and decoys;
  - the colossi's rise, the stoop, the unseen and the hidden.

## Efficiency (never at the cost of quality)

- **Build once, reuse everywhere:**
  - rig families (biped, hunched, quadruped, multi-legged, winged, serpent/chain, radial, colossus, formless, swarm), whose
    locomotion is generated from leg count, lengths and speed;
  - shared reactions, varied by parameters;
  - a procedural material library (scales, chitin, hide, fungal flesh, membrane, cloth, bone, slime, stone-skin);
  - a data-driven juice kit: per form, its socket emitters, hit flash, wind-up glow, trail, death, arrival and footfall.

  A form is then a recipe plus only what is its own. Hand-key its signature attacks and its idle.
- **Conflict-free by construction**, so batches can run as parallel sessions:
  - each form is one file, `src/data/creatures/<id>[.<variant>].ts` (its recipe and keyed clips), with its own card;
  - forms are found by `import.meta.glob`, not a hand-kept index;
  - `tools/creatures/status.mjs` computes the manifest's status;
  - generated tables are regenerated after a rebase, never hand-merged.
- **Shared code freezes after the kickoff.** A batch never edits the shared kits or the runtime: it works around in its
  form's own file and lists the kit change it wants in its report, and a `kit round` makes those changes between waves.
- **Tests are table-driven:** Vitest generates every converted form and checks its real geometry, weights and clips, so a
  batch adds data, not tests.
- **One review command:** `npm run creatures:review -- <ids>` writes one review page with the game's own renderer
  (Playwright). It holds the turnaround, every clip as a strip from the side and front, the budgets, QA, the in-realm
  captures and the chosen picture.
- **A fast inner loop:**
  - edit the recipe, let Vite reload, run the targeted vitest;
  - the full `npm run check` once per commit;
  - the slow audits with `AUDIT_ONLY=<ids>`, filtered to the batch and the fights that summon it while working, then in
    full once before the PR.
- **Agents:** one agent per form, each editing only its own files; the lead integrates, tests, reviews and commits. Don't
  spend an agent on what a script does.

## Card template

One file per form: `docs/creatures/<id>[.<variant>].md`.

- **Sources:** story, chapter, and short quotes checked against the text (Gutenberg or Wikisource if the network allows,
  else marked unchecked).
- **Anatomy and size**, and what is invented (canonLooks false where the text is thin).
- **The drawing as concept sheet:** what is kept, and where the text overrules it.
- **Body:** rig family, gait and idle character.
- **Materials**, and how they answer light (wet, chitin, membrane, fungal, cloth, bone).
- **The move set:** each attack as a library id plus overrides, with its telegraph pose and its juice.
- **Emitters, death and arrival.**
- **Sounds.**

Lovecraft's own fiction and revisions only (spec §3C): nothing from the RPGs, Derleth, films or other games. Every design is
original.

## Budgets

These are starting points. The kickoff measures them on the pilot and writes the real ones into the charter.

- **Triangles at LOD0:**
  - man-sized forms 8–12k;
  - large forms ≤25k;
  - bosses ≤50k;
  - colossi and Outer Gods ≤80k.

  LOD1 is about 40% of LOD0, and LOD2 about 15%. An impostor rendered from the model takes over beyond about 60 m and in
  skyLife.
- **Textures:**
  - 512² for man-sized forms, 1024² for large forms and bosses, 2048² for colossi;
  - ≤128 MB of creature textures resident.
- **Draws:** instanced skinning (bone matrices in a data texture), so each form costs about two draws per pass however many
  are on screen.
- **Frames:** PERFORMANCE.md's "smooth" verdict (55 fps average, 1% low ≥40) in every `?bench` place at the default
  settings, on the target hardware. A creature-detail setting (LOD bias, texture size) that autoQuality's ladder can step
  down carries weaker machines.
- **Load:** a realm's forms generate in ≤2 s, then come from the cache.
- **Size:** all recipes and clips together stay within a few hundred KB.

## Steps

### `kickoff` (batch 1)

**Read first:**
- docs: CREATURE_ART.md, ATTACK_FX.md, PERFORMANCE.md, and PLAYER.md (its clip DSL, markers and QA only);
- `src/data/`: schema.ts, attacks.ts, bossPunish.ts;
- `src/systems/`: creatures.ts, hurt.ts;
- `src/render/`: creatureViews.ts, worldMaterial.ts, worldLights.ts, textures.ts, gait.ts, autoQuality.ts;
- `tools/player/`: clipkit.py, solve.py.

A pre-production session may be writing the other forms' cards at the same time: write only the pilot's cards, and touch no
other card.

Define the rig, recipe, clip and material formats first. Then build in parallel agents:
- the modelling and texture kit;
- the rig and animation kit;
- the runtime and material side.

**Deliverables:**

a. **`docs/CREATURES_3D.md`**, the charter later steps obey, readable in a minute. It holds:
   - the manifest: id[#variant], tier, rig family and batch;
   - the batch order, the bar and the rules;
   - the measured budgets, and the definition of done;
   - a cookbook: one form from card to PR, command by command;
   - the before-numbers: `?bench` draws, triangles and frame times per place, bundle bytes, load time, GPU memory;
   - every consumer of the sprite art and what replaces it. Grep creatureViews.sheet, render/sprites, reviewed* and
     REVIEWED_*, and cover the cast shadows, tone, reveal, stoop, glow, matter, echoes, skyLife, ui/bestiary, ui/loading,
     and the ~30 tests that pin the art.

b. **`src/render/creatureKit/`:** the modelling, texture, rig, skinning, clip-DSL/solver, material and juice kits.

c. **The runtime path:**
   - a switch: Settings › Display › Creatures: Drawn | Modelled, plus `?models` and `?sprites`;
   - the creature-detail setting, and the picture setting with its three options;
   - every new label in en, de, fr and es;
   - instanced skinning, LODs and impostors.

   Drawn stays the default, and every sprite test stays green until the cut-over.

d. **The pilot**, each form complete to the bar: Deep One (and its eldritch form), Night-gaunt, Shoggoth and Cats of Ulthar
   (a biped, a flyer, a formless body and an ally). Each gets:
   - its card, move set, model with LODs, textures, rig, sockets and juice;
   - its sounds, re-synced to its clips;
   - every clip: idle; move at its stats.speed without sliding; each attack with markers on the data's frames; hurt,
     stagger, parried, death; and its ambush, flight or variant swap.

   The review page shows each one in the three pictures.

e. **Table-driven tests** in the everyday set, for every converted form:
   - a clip for every move it can make, with markers on the data's frames;
   - no NaN, weights that sum to one, loops that close, and feet that don't slide;
   - sockets (and zones) inside the body on every frame;
   - the same mesh from the same seed;
   - budgets met.

Note in the charter where the pilot's time went, and automate the slowest repeated step before you stop.

**Stop:** convert nothing beyond the pilot. Nothing more is converted until I approve the look and choose the picture.

### `pre-production`

Docs only, beside the kickoff. Touch no code, no data, and none of the pilot's cards: Deep One and its eldritch form,
Night-gaunt, Shoggoth and Cats of Ulthar.

1. **Write the cards.** Write `docs/creatures/<id>[.<variant>].md` for each of the other 107 forms in
   `src/data/roster.ts` (the enemies, their variants and the allies), to the card template above.
2. **Read first:** `src/data/entities/*.ts`, attacks.ts, archetypes.ts, bossPunish.ts and bestiaryNotes.ts;
   docs/CREATURE_ART.md; and the approved drawings in `public/creatures/reviewed` and `public/creatures/allies` (each
   index.json says where the frames are).
3. **Work in parallel:** one agent per tier or story group, each writing only its own cards.
4. **Review the roster as a whole**, which no single card can do, and fix what clashes:
   - no two forms look or move alike, unless the text makes them kin;
   - each tier's move sets differ in pace, reach and rhythm, and every telegraph stays readable;
   - a boss's phases escalate through what its anatomy can do;
   - every number proposed keeps the bossPunish bands, BOSS.blowMost and round 100's caps.
5. **End with one review page for me.** For each form: its drawing beside its card's summary, its move set at a glance, and
   where the text overrules the drawing. Then the questions only I can answer.

### `batch <k>`

Build the forms of batch k in the table below. The cards and move sets are designed and approved, so build them; change a
design only where building proves it wrong, and say why in the card.

- **Per form:** recipe → model, LODs and textures → rig and weights → every clip → sockets and juice → sounds → review strip.
- **Agents:** one agent per form; each edits only `src/data/creatures/<id>.ts` and its card.
- **Logging:** judgment calls go in the cards, plus one DECISIONS line for the batch.
- **A boss also gets:**
  - its arrival and fall, choreographed to its cutscene's camera beats (`data/cutscenes*`);
  - its phase changes and eldritch reveal, as transformations;
  - its signature's picture (spec §3E);
  - for a colossus, its stoop and zones.
- **Stop** after this batch.

### `kit round`

Make the kit and runtime changes the merged batch reports asked for. Regenerate every form they touch and review them on one
page. Leave the forms' own designs alone.

### `cut-over`

All 112 forms are Modelled and approved.

1. Make Modelled the only creature path. Remove `public/creatures` and the sprite tools; git history keeps them.
2. Apply any kit changes still pending from the batch reports, and regenerate what they touch.
3. Replace every consumer of the sprite art that the charter lists.
4. Retire a test that pins the drawn pack only once each guarantee it held has a 3D test: grounding, sides, the stoop,
   zones, shadows, tone, reveal, echoes, roof. Never weaken a test to make it pass.
5. Update:
   - SPEC §2 and CLAUDE.md;
   - CREATURE_ART.md, folded into CREATURES_3D.md;
   - the bestiary and loading;
   - the store and itch art, recaptured with the models.
6. Check:
   - `npm run check:all` and `npm run build`;
   - a full `npm run audit`, with the balance tables reviewed;
   - `?bench` in every place, against the charter's before-numbers: draws, triangles, frame times, bundle bytes, load time,
     GPU memory.
7. Report what changed for players, and what is still unverified on the target hardware.
