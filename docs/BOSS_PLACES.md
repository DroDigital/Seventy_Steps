# The outdoor boss places (round 71)

Each of the nineteen open arenas, the horrors that fight in the open world and not in a dungeon, stands in a place of its own: a court walled and framed by models from the
supplied asset collection (eighteen packs), pieces made in code where the packs had no form for it, and sources of mist, sparks, flame and wisps set at the things that would
give them off. The fight itself is unchanged: the same centres, radii, bosses, summons, rewards and saves, and the gates still open freely (no puzzle, no key, no discovery lock).

- Which models are used, from which pack, from which source file, and where they stand: [BOSS_MODELS.md](BOSS_MODELS.md) (generated by `python3 -I tools/models/boss/mapping.py`).
- Licences and the changes made to the CC BY packs: [`src/data/models/CREDITS.md`](../src/data/models/CREDITS.md).
- The judgement calls: `docs/DECISIONS.md`, "Round 71".

## How it is built

- **Models are baked offline.** `tools/models/bake_boss_models.py <work dir>` reads each source with Blender's own importers (`pip install bpy numpy pillow`; the tool's dependency, not the game's), applies the manifest
  entry (`tools/models/boss/manifest_pNN.py`: the objects that are the model, its turning and size, what each material is, what a body cannot walk through, where a flame sits), and writes
  `src/data/models/bossIndex.ts` and `bossPack.ts` (16-bit points, flat-shaded faces, the class of each material: stone, wood, leaf, rock, slab, cobble, brick, cloth, metal, bone, glow) beside the Kenney set
  (`kenneyIndex.ts`, `kenneyPack.ts`), which it never touches. `tools/models/boss/unpack.py <collection> <work dir>` unpacks the archives first (each in its own directory;
  the scripts live elsewhere; every Python that reads them runs `-I`). Forms with no source are made in code (`proc.py`, `proc_more.py`, `proc_grove.py`: trunks, roots, pods, prisms, pylons, fangs, masks, a hull, a mast).
  A run without `--only` rebakes everything from the manifests, so the result is the same each time.
- **A place is an enclosure of the kind `place`.** `src/world/places/<boss>.ts` is its planner: it lays pieces in the court's frame (`world/placeKit.ts`: `a` ahead along the way the arena faces, `c` to the left;
  `add`, `run`, `ring`, `emit`) and says the outline of its wall; `world/enclosurePlace.ts` makes of that an enclosure like any other (its gate, stake, candle, rods, pad of levelled ground, closure). The colour-out-of-space
  basin and the black river keep their own enclosures from #157 and #161 and a planner enriches them (`EXTRAS`).
- **Colliders are decomposed, never one box to a building.** Each model's `solids` are boxes about its walls and posts (`'auto'` voxelises a doorway or colonnade so it stays open; `'box'` for a solid mass such as a chapel;
  a piece with `walk: true` has none). `tests/enclosure.test.ts` floods the land to prove nothing walks in but by the gate, `tests/bossPlaces.test.ts` keeps the fight's ring clear and the way from the gate to it open,
  and `tests/enclosedAudit.test.ts` finds any room a body could enter and not leave.
- **Drawing.** A piece is a prop of the kind `set`, drawn by the same batched instancing the arenas' sets use, in the realm's grade, with no new material. Doors are drawn by `render/gateWild.ts` and `gateMetal.ts`
  (roots, fangs, leaves, portcullis, bronze leaves) and open as a body nears. What breathes is drawn by `render/courtLife.ts` from `Emit`s anchored at a source (`world/placeEmit.ts`).
- **Photographs.** `node tools/boss_shots.mjs <out dir> --views approach,gate,high [--info stats.jsonl] [boss ...]` (needs Playwright and the dev server).

## Substitutions and deviations

- **Pack 07** (Jen Abbott's Egyptian Temple Complex, Fab Standard License) is not baked, shipped or committed: its licence does not allow the raw assets to be republished, and a baked model carries them.
  The Colossus's temenos uses pack 08's sandstone temple, pack 09's ruins and pylons, obelisks and sand made in code. `tests/bossPlaces.test.ts` fails if any manifest names the pack.
- **Pack 12** was not needed: Azathoth's dancers are four poses of pack 13's base meshes (heads removed, an eyeless egg set on the neck, thirteen different proportions).
- **Pack 18** (a dungeon's stone) supplies only the Ancient Ones' threshold steps: a hexagonal court of pale prisms is not a dungeon's masonry, and the other nine models were not stood anywhere,
  so they are not baked (`tests/arenaSets.test.ts` allows no model baked and unused). 93 models of the first bake were dropped on those grounds.
- **Zkauba** uses pack 11 with the colony's markings thrown away (`recolour`) so that its towers read as the pale metal-stone of Yaddith, not a modern base.
- **Hastur's calling** is untouched; a Hastur called to the ruins still rises there, and when that fight ends without him he goes home to the theatre, where his arena now is (`tests/greatOldOnes.test.ts`).
- **Chapels, doorways, domes:** a chapel is a solid mass whose doorway is drawn, not walked (a room no body can leave is forbidden); the laboratory's door is cut 1.9 m wide.

The counts are the renderer's for the whole frame (the place, the land and sky about it, the sea, the creatures), which is why they are of the order of 300,000 triangles whatever the place. The pieces of a place alone are 8,000 to 77,000 triangles (mean 23,000; `tests/bossPlaces.test.ts` holds each under 110,000 and the mean under 60,000). A place replaces the plain ring house and its set, so the frame does not grow on the whole: the mean gate view is 341,000 → 317,000 triangles and 106 → 103 draw calls; the places that grow are the ones that are most built (the Daemon Pipers +31%, Shub-Niggurath +12%, the Colour Out of Space +4%), the worst frame of the three views is 557,000 triangles before and 581,000 after (the gate of the Colour Out of Space in both, +4%), while the most draw calls in any frame fall (185 → 177). (The renderer's geometry and texture counters are those of the page's caches and only climb as one place after another is photographed, so they are not compared.) Pieces are drawn by the same batched instancing as the arenas' sets, and a place's sources of mist, sparks and wisps emit only while the camera is within their reach (110–120 m) and spawn through the game's existing particle pool; the only thing built per birth is its small spec object, as every other effect's.

Load: the baked models are 0.78 MB of data in the bundle (the Kenney set's 0.29 MB is unchanged); decoding every model there is (226: the Kenney 59 and the 167 of the places) into pieces takes about 0.33–0.44 s, against 0.21–0.23 s for the 59 before; reading the pack module takes 23–29 ms against 7 ms. The models decode lazily, so in play only those of the places near are decoded as their chunks are built (CPU time in Node).
The production bundle (`npm run build`): the main chunk 2,158 kB → 2,233 kB (gzip 726 → 749 kB); the model data is its own lazily loaded chunk, 291 kB → 1,072 kB (gzip 134 → 499 kB), read as the world is made.

## Tests

New: `tests/bossPlaces.test.ts` (nineteen places each with their packs, each its own, the fight's ring clear and the way in open, gate on the wall and stake within it, triangle budgets, sources that breathe, pack 07 absent, every pack credited and the CC BY changes named).
Adapted, not weakened: `arenaHouse`, `enclosure`, `basinRiver`, `arenaStyles` and `bailey` (a place's models are not the plain house's pieces: the height and pad rules hold for the `house_*` walls, and the models are asked by `bossPlaces`);
`arenaSets` (the no-dead-weight rule counts what a planner stands); `arenaWays` (a place's court begins past its levelled ground); `houseWalk` (an audit left stale by round 69: the gate side now asserted to come in, as its title says).
Results: `npm run check:all` (typecheck, the everyday set and the audits: the soak, the bots that fight every boss and creature, `bossStand`, the dungeon, roof, outside and object glitch audits, `enclosedAudit`, `houseWalk`, `balance`): 334 files, 3,423 tests, all pass; `npm run build` passes.

## Not verified, and limits

- Real frame times and the look on a GPU: the container has none. The pictures are of a software renderer (flatter than a GPU, no bloom stalls).
- The places were photographed in the gloaming with a little added light; their look in the full dark of the night and in weather was judged from the same scenes, not photographed for each.
- The fights themselves were not replayed by a person in each place: the bots of `bossBot`/`bossStand` fight the bosses in them, and the tests prove the ring is clear and the way in open.
- Cutscenes: each horror's arrival and fall cameras (`cinema*`) are unchanged and were not re-reviewed for the new walls beyond the fog the cameras already keep inside (`cinemaFog`).
