# Automobile mount: 1890–1940 reference research

Researched 17 October 2026. The corrected user brief covers automobiles from **1890–1940**. Select one coherent period within that range; these decades do not share one body style.

## Selected visual direction: circa 1914 touring car

Create an original **Brassbound Motor Car inspired by the 1914 touring era**, rather than labeling it a museum replica. Interpret the linked reference in the game's charcoal, bone and tarnished-metal palette:

- Tall, upright narrow radiator; readable cap and fine grille; a distinct hood behind it.
- Separate round lamps, exposed spoke wheels, curved individual mudguards and full running boards.
- Open touring cabin, upright split windscreen and fabric canopy supported by slender bows.
- Black enamel body, worn dark upholstery and selective aged brass around the radiator and lamps. Keep the windscreen frame and steering hardware mostly dark: the museum notes steel replaced several brass fittings for 1913. [1913 Model T catalog](https://www.thehenryford.org/collections/explore/artifact/231335).
- Make the axle, springs, wheel rotation and steering legible; add restrained chassis pitch, body roll, canopy movement and engine vibration. These animation choices are an interpretation for this game.
- Use warm lamp light and modest weathering to support the gothic setting. Avoid badges, modern instruments or chrome-heavy decoration. Ford introduced electric Model T headlights for 1915, so a strict 1914 interpretation should use the earlier lamp appearance. [Museum lamp history](https://www.thehenryford.org/collections/explore/artifact/388590).

The sources establish dates, materials, mechanical distinctions and reference images. The procedural mesh, palette and animations remain original work. Historical engine specifications are reference context; **both mounts retain the same gameplay acceleration, speed, handling, stamina rules and AI noise**.
